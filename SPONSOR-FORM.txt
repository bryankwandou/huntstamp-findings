Thirty findings filed separately through the Typeform, from three sweeps.

Findings 1-17 were observed against deployment dpl_74W5o38E8FQjJUZ2DYo5PFDHEsLs on
3 September 2026, Chromium 148 on Windows 11, at 1440x900 and 375x812. Manic shipped
dpl_C3bXHquKbKr1sjn9WpRtDt8MWfce partway through the session, so every one of those was
re-checked against the newer build by an automated pass. Findings 18-31 were taken on
4 September against the newer build. Screenshots and raw measurement files for all of it
are published alongside the report.

THE FOUR WITH THE MOST WEIGHT

1. The trading interface renders inside a cross-origin iframe. Neither X-Frame-Options nor
   a CSP frame-ancestors directive is sent on any route. A page served from an unrelated
   origin embedded /pm and the frame attached, loaded and painted the live market grid,
   prices and Log In button, with no objection from the browser. I demonstrated the framing
   with a screenshot. I did not build a clickjacking exploit and I am not claiming one - but
   on a venue where a click places an order, this is a control worth closing.

2. Settlement information is wrong on settled markets, and it is not a one-off. A market
   marked Resolved, order book closed, final score 2-6 on screen, advertises Settlement
   Time: Sep 9, 3:00 PM UTC for a match played on 2 September. Sampling 45 markets whose
   slug carries a match date, 15 of them - one in three - advertise a close date exactly
   seven days after the match, with 28 correct and nothing in between one and seven days.
   The same wrong date is served in the og:description, so shared links advertise settled
   markets as still open.

3. /pm stops resolving to the market directory once any event has been opened. The cause is
   localStorage 'pm-events-store', which restores the stored event and rewrites the address
   bar. Removing that key restores the directory, so the root cause is isolated rather than
   just the symptom. This is the entry point named in the bounty brief, so anyone following
   a documentation link to /pm lands somewhere else.

4. The directory downloads 17.4 MB and 8,610 event objects to render 24 cards. It requests
   limit=500 for fourteen category tags and paginates each through offset 0, 500 and 1000,
   on first load, while the user is looking at Trending. That is 359 events fetched per card
   displayed.

THE REST, IN BRIEF

Routing: any invented market slug returns HTTP 200 with your generic landing-page title
instead of 404, while neighbouring routes 404 correctly; /PM returns 404 where /pm returns
200; every 404 ships 1.44 MB of application HTML.

Metadata: the page title and Open Graph tags are emitted at the very end of a 1.5 MB
document - on 60 of 60 sampled pages the title sits beyond 256 KB, and on 13 of them after
</head> has closed. A Slackbot-agent request capped at 256 KB receives no title and no
description at all.

Accessibility: no heading elements anywhere on /pm or the event pages, and two <main>
landmarks per page; two <h1> elements on /referral against one on the root and none on /pm;
21 of 23 images on the directory without an alt attribute; four controls on the landing page
with no accessible name; zero-sized controls from the inactive responsive layout left in the
keyboard tab order.

Security hygiene: no CSP, X-Content-Type-Options, Referrer-Policy, Permissions-Policy or
Cross-Origin-Opener-Policy on any response; the is-mobile cookie carries neither Secure nor
HttpOnly. HSTS is present and http:// redirects correctly, so the foundations are there.

Display: a resolved market promoted in Trending with live buy buttons; prop labels that
repeat the full event title so the rail truncates exactly where the identifying text begins;
"-- Vol" where $0 belongs; the same candidate spelled two ways on two cards visible at once;
a category click that silently discards the search query; zero-count filters offered as
selectable; the August rewards promotion still advertised in September on the landing page,
the leaderboard and the referral page.

WHAT I WITHDREW

Two findings were withdrawn rather than filed. Both are documented on the report rather than
deleted, because you should see what I got wrong before you weigh what I got right.

- I measured the /pm load event at 94s and then 136s through an instrumented browser and had
  written it up as a performance defect. An automated run on the same machine and connection
  then loaded the same route with loadEventEnd at 1,581ms and TTFB at 57ms. The slow figures
  were an artefact of my environment, not your application. What the evidence does support is
  filed instead as the 17.4 MB payload above, which measures response bodies rather than
  wall-clock time and so does not depend on my machine.

- Mobile subcategory chips appeared non-functional. Re-testing showed the cause was my
  automation harness.

One filed finding has its frequency answered honestly as "only happened once": a settled
market rendering two contradictory price sets, one pair summing to 101%. The capture is
verbatim and the build id is recorded, but it did not reproduce on re-test, so triage should
expect that. A second is filed the same way: one market whose description reads "Completed
Match 50% - Ends Sep 11", seen once in a 60-page sample and not found again in a targeted
re-scan of 45.

I also checked several things that turned out to be sound, and I have published those too:
search rejects markup and SQL-shaped input cleanly with nothing executed; browsing produced
zero console errors and zero failing requests across four routes; all eight complementary
price pairs on the directory sum to exactly 100; every visible navigation link resolves; and
canonical, og:image and twitter:card are present on all 60 sampled pages.

WHAT I DID NOT TEST

This ran entirely as a logged-out guest. No funds were deposited and no orders were placed.
Deposits, order placement, order matching, positions, balances, withdrawals and settlement
crediting all require a funded account with real USDC at risk, and none of it was exercised.
That is where this programme's P0 weight sits, and it remains untested. I would be glad to
run the same pass against a funded account if that is useful.

Testing was AI-driven browser automation rather than manual QA. Stated up front, because
every finding rests on reproduction steps your own team can run, and the capture scripts are
in the repository so you can re-run any measurement rather than taking mine on trust.
