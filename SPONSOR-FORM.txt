All findings were first observed against deployment dpl_74W5o38E8FQjJUZ2DYo5PFDHEsLs on
3 September 2026, Chromium 148 on Windows 11, at 1440x900 and 375x812. Manic shipped a
newer build, dpl_C3bXHquKbKr1sjn9WpRtDt8MWfce, partway through the session, and every
finding was then re-checked against it by an automated Playwright pass. The screenshots
and raw measurements from that pass are published alongside the report.

Sixteen findings filed separately through the Typeform.

The two with the most weight, both re-confirmed on the newer build:

- A market marked Resolved, with its order book closed and a final score of 2-6 on
  screen, still advertises Settlement Time: Sep 9, 3:00 PM UTC. The match was played
  on 2 September, which the event slug itself records. A user checking when funds are
  released is given a date with no relation to a settlement that already happened.

- /pm stops resolving to the market directory once any event has been opened. The cause
  is localStorage 'pm-events-store', which restores the stored event and rewrites the
  address bar. Removing that key restores the directory, so the root cause is isolated
  rather than just the symptom. This is the entry point named in the bounty brief, so
  anyone following a documentation link to /pm lands somewhere else.

Two findings were withdrawn rather than filed. Both withdrawals are documented in the
report rather than deleted:

- Mobile subcategory chips appeared non-functional. Re-testing showed the cause was my
  automation harness, not the application.

- I measured the /pm load event at 94s and then 136s through an instrumented browser and
  had written it up as a performance defect. An automated run on the same machine and
  connection then loaded the same route with loadEventEnd at 1,581ms and TTFB at 57ms.
  The slow figures were an artefact of my environment. The route does request 115 JS
  chunks, but that is an observation, not a defect on this evidence.

One further finding is filed with its frequency answered honestly as "only happened
once": a settled market rendering two contradictory price sets, one pair summing to
101%. The capture is verbatim and the build id is recorded, but it did not reproduce on
re-test, so triage should expect that.

This session ran entirely as a logged-out guest. No funds were deposited and no orders
were placed. Deposits, order placement, positions, balances and settlement crediting all
require a funded account with real USDC at risk, and none of that was exercised. That is
where this programme's P0 weight sits, and it remains untested.

Testing was AI-driven browser automation rather than manual QA. Stated up front, because
every finding rests on reproduction steps your own team can run.
